# Working notes: L-meta-19037 by hand

**Claim (L-meta-19037, weight 120, truth unknown).** For every n ≥ 2 and every n×n bipartite graph A:
if some AND/OR circuit of fan-in two with s gates represents the complement Ā on the one-a-side promise,
then some such circuit with at most s + 2n gates represents A. In short, m(A) ≤ m(Ā) + 2n.

Setting: the inputs are the row wires r_0..r_{n−1} and the column wires c_0..c_{n−1}. On the promise exactly one row
wire (row u) and one column wire (column v) are on. Every gate counts; an AND of no wires is the constant true.

The project's notes (the step's sketch):
- **Slope 2 is needed.** A single cell costs 1 gate and its complement exactly 2n − 3, a gap of 2n − 4.
- **The sharper conjecture** (L-meta-19385) is m(A) − m(Ā) ≤ 2n − 4. The tables at sides 3 and 4 never exceed it, and
  our own 3×3 and 4×4 runs agree: the largest gap is exactly 2n − 4.
- **The checked transfer** L-meta-18925 gives only about 2·m(Ā) + 6n.

---

## 1. On the promise, a circuit is a set expression

On the promise an input is a cell (u, v). Each gate therefore computes a set of cells:
- the row wire r_i gives the row R_i = {i} × [n], and the column wire c_j gives the column K_j = [n] × {j};
- OR is union and AND is intersection;
- the constant true is the whole grid and the constant false is the empty set.

So a circuit represents A exactly when its output gate computes A as a set.

## 2. The duality bound: m(A) ≤ m(Ā) + 6n − 12 for n ≥ 3 (proved here, not yet checked in Lean)

Let C represent Ā with s gates. Build C* by swapping every AND with OR and every OR with AND. This also turns the
constant true into the constant false. Feed C* the wires
  y_i = OR of all row wires except r_i, and z_j = OR of all column wires except c_j.

**Claim:** C* fed these wires represents A.

**Proof.** On the promise, y_i computes the complement of R_i (all rows but i), and z_j the complement of K_j. Take
any gate of C, computing the set X, and let X* be the set its copy in C* computes. Show by induction that
X* = complement of X:
- At an input: true by the choice of y_i and z_j.
- At an OR gate: (X ∪ Y)ᶜ = Xᶜ ∩ Yᶜ, and C* has an AND there.
- At an AND gate: (X ∩ Y)ᶜ = Xᶜ ∪ Yᶜ, and C* has an OR there.
- At the constants: true and false swap, as complements should.

So the output of C* is (Ā)ᶜ = A. ∎

**Cost.** C* has s gates. The n wires y_i, all computed together, cost at most 3n − 6 gates: that is L-meta-18924,
proved by green-notes ("the n ORs of all inputs but one are computed together by at most 3n − 6 gates"). The same
holds for the columns. Total: s + 6n − 12.

This is already stronger than the checked L-meta-18925 (no factor 2 on m(Ā)), but the slope is 6 against the
claimed 2.

## 3. Refinement: complement whole row blocks, not single wires

Inside C, look at the part that reads only row wires. On the promise, any such sub-circuit computes "row u is in S"
for some set of rows S.
- The original computes R_S = ∪_{i∈S} R_i; the dual needs its complement R_{S^c}.
- R_{S^c} costs |S^c| − 1 gates directly (an OR tree over the rows not in S), so there is no need to go through the
  y_i at all.
- So: replace each row block by a fresh computation of its complement, do the same for column blocks, and dualize
  only the gates that mix rows and columns.

**Example: the single cell.**
- C = AND(r_0, c_0), so its blocks are {0} and {0}.
- C* = OR(R_{rows≠0}, K_{cols≠0}). That costs (n − 2) + (n − 2) + 1 = 2n − 3, which is the exact value. The
  overhead is 2n − 4, exactly the conjectured worst case.

**Cost in general.** Let the blocks C actually uses form a family 𝒮 of row sets and a family 𝒯 of column sets.
Write cost(𝒮) for the least number of gates that computes every set in 𝒮 from the single rows, using unions and
intersections. Then
  m(A) ≤ s − cost(𝒮) − cost(𝒯) + cost(𝒮ᶜ) + cost(𝒯ᶜ),
where 𝒮ᶜ is the family of complements. So the claim would follow from **cost(𝒮ᶜ) ≤ cost(𝒮) + n for every family 𝒮**,
and that would give exactly +2n.

## 4. The obstacle: that family lemma is false

Take 𝒮 = all n single rows. It is free (cost 0, they are the inputs), but its complements are the n "all but one"
unions, which cost 3n − 6 (L-meta-18924's construction; that count is believed optimal).
- It shows up in practice: the matching (u = v) is represented by OR_i (r_i ∧ c_i), reading every single row and
  column. Dualizing gives the crown, at cost about (2n − 1) + 2(3n − 6), roughly 8n.
- But the crown actually costs at most 3n + 4⌈√n⌉ − 9 (L-meta-18153 with L-meta-18183), well inside the claim's
  m(matching) + 2n = 4n − 1. So duality is very wasteful exactly when C reads many single rows or columns.

**So any proof of the claim needs a second construction for that regime.** Plan from here:
1. Write the cost of the dualized circuit in terms of how many single rows and columns C reads. With k rows read:
   a common "rest" OR plus an all-but-one network over the k gives about n + 2k − 5 for that side.
   - The claim follows outright whenever C reads few singletons, about k_rows + k_cols ≤ 5.
2. For circuits that read many singletons, look for a construction that uses C's structure: each single row read
   costs C at least one gate somewhere. Can that be traded against the extra negation cost?
3. Use the 4×4 and 5×5 tables: for the graphs with the largest gaps m(A) − m(Ā), look at optimal circuits for both
   A and Ā and check which regime they fall in.

## 5. What the 4×4 table shows (computed with the solver, not checked in Lean)

**The extremes are partial matchings against their complements.** The largest gap at 4×4 is 2n − 4 = 4. It is
reached by:
- a single cell against its complement;
- a cross (one row plus one column) against the rectangle it leaves out;
- matchings of 2 and 3 cells against their complements.

For a matching M_k of k cells: m(M_k) = 2k − 1 and m(complement) = 2n + 2k − 5, a gap of exactly 2n − 4 every time.
The complement splits as
- the rows the matching misses (one OR),
- plus the columns it misses (one OR),
- plus a k×k crown on the matched block.

**Reduction lemma (proved on paper).** If Ā has empty rows or columns, those are full in A, and
  A = OR(full rows) ∪ OR(full columns) ∪ A',
where A' is A on the block U×W of rows and columns that Ā touches. Every row and column of that block must be
read by any circuit for Ā. The bookkeeping gives: the claim for A follows from the claim for the block, with |U| + |W|
in place of 2n. **So it is enough to prove the claim when Ā touches every row and column.**

That restricted case still reaches the gap 2n − 4 at 4×4, so the reduction does not remove the extremes. The crown is
the prime suspect.

## 6. The crown: a sharp target at n = 5

- **3×3:** the crown costs 7 and the matching 5, a gap of exactly 2n − 4.
- **4×4:** the crown lies between 9 and 11 (not settled yet); the matching costs 7.
- **5×5:** the matching costs 2n − 1 = 9. What is known about the crown:
  - it needs at least 3n = 15 gates (L-meta-18163);
  - it needs at most 18 (L-meta-18153 with L-meta-18183).
- **If the 5×5 crown needs 16 or more gates**, then m(crown) − m(matching) ≥ 7 > 2n − 4. That breaks L-meta-19385's
  conjecture, exactly where its notes say a break must live: "side 5 or more".
  - Proving it in Lean would need a certified lower bound of 16 for a 10-input instance. That is likely too large for
    the solver-certificate route (the file-size cap), so it would need a structural proof or a search engine.
- **If a 15-gate circuit exists**, the gap at n = 5 is exactly 2n − 4 for the crown too.
- Search started 22:1x local time: 4 seeds at 15 gates (an hour each) and 2 seeds at 16 gates (30 minutes each);
  outputs in WSL ~/ns/crown15_*.txt and crown16_*.txt.

## 7. How the four conjectures hang together (proved on paper)

**Fact:** g(A) ≤ m(Ā) + 1 for every graph. Take an optimal AND/OR circuit for Ā and put one NOT on top.

Consequences:
- **L-meta-19158 (NOT removal: m(A) ≤ g(A) + 2n − 5) implies m(A) ≤ m(Ā) + 2n − 4.** That is the statement that
  L-meta-19385 tries to break. So a single graph with m(A) > m(Ā) + 2n − 4 breaks both 19158 and the 2n − 4 gap
  conjecture, which proves both L-meta-19159 and L-meta-19385.
- **The cheapest route to 19159** (not held by anyone, weight 120): a 5×5 graph A whose AND/OR/NOT cost g(A) is at most
  m_lower − 6, where m_lower is an AND/OR lower bound already proved.
  - For the 5×5 crown, m ≥ 15 is already on main (L-meta-18163). So an AND/OR/NOT circuit of 9 gates or fewer for
    the crown would prove 19159, with no new lower-bound proof at all.
  - NOT on top of the matching gives 10. At 4×4 the crown with NOT costs exactly m(matching) + 1 = 8, and NOT
    does not help the matching (g = m = 7). So 9 at 5×5 is unlikely but cheap to test; search started 22:3x.
- **If the crown with NOT costs 10:** 19159 needs m(crown_5) ≥ 16, the same question as in section 6.

## 8. The crown costs at most 4n − 5 (proved; circuit verified on every input for n = 3..8)

**Construction.** Put x_i = r_i ∨ c_i (n gates). On the promise, exactly the x's at u and v are on, so "at least two
x's are on" is exactly u ≠ v. The threshold "at least two of n" has an AND/OR circuit of 3n − 5 gates:
- suffix ORs S_i = x_i ∨ S_{i+1} for i = n−2 down to 1, with S_{n−1} = x_{n−1} (n − 2 gates);
- the terms x_i ∧ S_{i+1} for i = 0..n−2 (n − 1 gates);
- an OR of the terms (n − 2 gates).

Total **crown(n) ≤ 4n − 5**: 7, 11, 15, 19, 23, 27 for n = 3..8 (all checked by evaluation on the promise).

**Consequences:**
- **crown(5) = 15 exactly**, together with L-meta-18163 (crown ≥ 3n for n ≥ 5). The best construction on record
  (L-meta-18153 with L-meta-18183, 3n + 4⌈√n⌉ − 9) gave 18.
- **The bound beats the known one at n = 5, 6, 7** (15, 19, 23 against 18, 21, 24), ties at n = 8, and loses from
  n = 9 on.
- **The crown never exceeds the conjectured gap:** m(crown) − m(matching) ≤ (4n − 5) − (2n − 1) = 2n − 4.
- **Neither do the partial matchings:** the complement of a k-cell matching costs
  ≤ 2n − 2k + crown(k) ≤ 2n + 2k − 5, against m(M_k) = 2k − 1, a gap ≤ 2n − 4.
- **So the extremal family at 4×4 is capped at exactly 2n − 4 for every n.** A break of L-meta-19385 must come from a
  different kind of graph. The 5×5 table run is the place to look.
- **For L-meta-19159 the crown would need g(crown_5) ≤ 9.** NOT on top of the matching gives 10. That search is still
  running.
- **For L-meta-19037 (the 2n claim) this family is fine:** the gap is 2n − 4 ≤ 2n.
